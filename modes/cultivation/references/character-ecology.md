# Character ecology

## Detail tiers

- Tier A, Anchor: complete dossier, 2–4 long arcs, complex secrets, independent career, historical agency.
- Tier B, Major: complete personality and social position, one main arc, medium agenda.
- Tier C, Contextual Formal: stable identity, occupation, relationships, goal, contradiction; deepen through play.

A useful 150-person default is 30 A, 60 B, 60 C. Change counts to fit the world, but preserve uneven detail.

## Required variety

Cover successful and failed talents; late bloomers; injured and aging cultivators; artisans, teachers, healers, jurists, historians, merchants and researchers; political actors; legendary rogues; rational or idealist demonic cultivators; nonhuman institutions; and young talents whose careers do not orbit the player.

## Individuality test

Sample at least 50 new characters. Compare:

`surface personality + deep personality + current goal + cultivation style + occupation + faction function`

If many fingerprints differ only by name, element, or realm, redesign the new characters. Never modify old canon to make room.

## Relationships

Give every formal NPC 2–5 meaningful edges. Form local clusters by faction, place, profession, family, or shared history. Use only a few bridge actors. Relationships must have a world cause.

## Attraction and romance

Character appeal comes from intelligence, conviction, contradiction, vulnerability, humor, competence, ambition, kindness, cruelty, loyalty, obsession, worldview, failure, history, and relationships. NPCs may love, marry, refuse, divorce, grieve, remain single, betray, or repair relationships independently of the player.

## Demonic social order

For each demonic organization, explain member benefits, recorded rights and debts, betrayal costs, apprentice reproduction, outsider services, and succession. If there is no institutional answer, the faction is an enemy label rather than a civilization.
