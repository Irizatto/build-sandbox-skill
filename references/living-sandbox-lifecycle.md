# Living Sandbox Lifecycle

Use this lifecycle when the requested product is meant to sustain open-ended play, persistent actors, offscreen change, long-running relationships, expanding geography/topology, or hundreds of sessions. It is optional for a narrow one-off rules module.

The lifecycle is domain-neutral. “World horizon” can mean geography, a sports league/circuit, political jurisdictions, a social network, a management ecosystem, or any larger topology the player may enter over time.

## Six gated phases

### Phase 1 — Horizon / Topology Foundation

Goal: the sandbox must continue beyond the initial vertical slice without improvising permanent reality from nothing.

Build:
- stable macro topology and adjacency;
- aggregate state for offscreen areas;
- latent region/ecosystem seeds;
- deterministic seed → materialize → Canon transaction;
- stable IDs, provenance, versioning, save/reload identity;
- sparse context projection.

Do not pre-author hundreds of shallow places merely to simulate size.

### Phase 2 — Organization / Institution Ecology

Goal: organizations exist because resources, authority, membership, history, and incentives sustain them.

Build:
- latent organization seeds;
- organization families with different formation/governance/succession rules;
- founding, growth, decline, split, merger, migration, dissolution, legacy;
- offices separate from office holders;
- resource/economic dependencies;
- player-founded organizations under the same world rules.

### Phase 3 — Actor Promotion / Character Life

Goal: a contextual stranger can become important through history without the player's attention rewriting their destiny.

Build:
- contextual/recurring/major detail tiers;
- separate world importance, simulation importance, current narrative relevance, and player bookmark;
- conservative promotion that preserves existing facts and unknowns;
- everyday/private life, voice, goals, routines, social circles and pressures;
- fade/reactivation without identity loss;
- sparse actor projection.

Bookmark/favorite may increase retention and detail. It must not automatically increase power, fame, affection, luck, availability, plot armor, or cosmic importance.

### Phase 4 — Relationship / Narrative Runtime

Goal: relationships become accumulated history and scenes stay alive without random drama injection.

Build:
- multidimensional, potentially asymmetric relationships;
- causal relationship events, evidence, boundaries, hysteresis and public/private layers;
- Character Anchors;
- scene state, repetition/stagnation signals;
- causal beat proposals validated by the authoritative runtime;
- NPC initiative and deferred/offscreen plot states.

`Nothing important happens` is a valid scene outcome.

### Phase 5 — Domain Depth / Wonder / Affordance Ecology

Goal: deepen what makes the selected domain worth exploring and playing.

For cultivation this may mean technique ecology, strange geography, professions, spiritual ecology and opportunity windows. For competitive worlds it may mean distinct leagues, teams, staff cultures, travel, careers, training environments, meta adaptation and media ecosystems. For another domain, identify its own mechanically consequential sources of variety.

Do not substitute quantity of names for differentiated gameplay.

### Phase 6 — Long-Session Gameplay Hardening

Goal: prove the systems remain coherent and interesting after novelty wears off.

Run mundane-life, repeated-actor, absence, long-skip, relationship-stress, anti-protagonist, weird-action, save/reload, retrieval and corrupted-fixture tests. Compare short, medium and long-session experience. Route major defects back to their owner phase.

Automated simulation is evidence, not human playtesting. Do not call the build Final until human long-session gates are honestly satisfied.

## Phase gate rule

Every phase must define:
- IN SCOPE;
- INTERFACE ONLY;
- OUT OF SCOPE;
- authority changes;
- schema/version changes;
- migration/rollback behavior;
- deterministic and adversarial tests;
- context/retrieval impact;
- acceptance gates;
- handoff to the next phase.

Do not smuggle later-phase implementation into an earlier phase. Record it as deferred work.

## Domain adaptation

### Generic persistent worlds
Use all six phases when the world is open-ended and long-running.

### Competitive worlds
Use Phase 1 as competition-ecosystem/topology expansion when the product includes leagues, teams, cities, circuits, career movement, travel or social life. Use Phase 3–4 when players/coaches/staff are persistent characters rather than mere stat rows. Narrow draft/rules tools may skip unrelated living-world phases.

### Cultivation worlds
Use all six phases by default for large open-world builds.

## Completion question

Before declaring a living sandbox mature, answer:

> If the player ignores the intended content, travels elsewhere, becomes attached to an ordinary person, refuses major hooks, disappears for years, and later returns, does the same world still exist, with plausible independent history and bounded context?

If not, the sandbox is not yet fully living.
